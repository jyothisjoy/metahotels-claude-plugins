# Channel playbooks

Sources: Hormozi's Leads, Hooks/GOATed Ads, Lead Nurture, Marketing Machine, LTV/Retention, Fast Cash (notes 03, 06, 07, 09, 10, 11); Agora Books Two and Three (notes 15, 18: soap-opera sequences, gauntlets, bouncebacks, petitions, IKEA effect, newsjacking, SEO content, events). **[MH]** marks adaptations, which are inference.

## Paid ads (Hormozi's ad assembly line)
- **Ad = Hook (call-out + condition for value) + Meat + CTA.** Prep effort splits 80% hooks, 20% meat, and almost nothing on the CTA, which should simply be clear.
- **Meat formats:**
  - demonstration (room walk-through, before/after);
  - testimonial (guest UGC, a real review read aloud);
  - education ("3 things to know before booking Eid in Dubai");
  - story;
  - faceless (review screenshots, slideshow).
- **CTA: clear beats clever.** Say what to do, how, what they get and what happens next, and show the booking step.
- **Hook sources**, in order:
  1. your own past winners;
  2. your best organic content;
  3. others' ads;
  4. high-view content;
  5. ad libraries.
- **Volume vs sample size.** Hormozi tests 150+ ads a week on big budgets. On small hotel budgets, test **fewer** variants so each reaches a readable sample (CLAUDE.md gate). Note the conflict when proposing a test plan.
- **Value angles (What, Who, When):**
  - *What:* dream or nightmare, likelihood or risk, speed or slowness, ease or effort.
  - *Who:* status in the eyes of spouse, kids, friends.
  - *When:* past, present or future.
- Ogilvy: "The customer isn't a moron. She's your wife."

### Lead-Gen Scrambler: banks to recombine (Hormozi, Gym Launch ch 3; [MH] hotel versions)
Build variants by recombining one component at a time. These names are the **angle tags** used in `6_Campaigns/Ad_Swipe_File.md`.
- **Headline angles:**
  - `duration` ("3 nights, everything included");
  - `benefit`;
  - `status` (how they'll be seen);
  - `curiosity`;
  - `against-fear` (the thing they dread: the pool-chair race, a hidden resort fee);
  - `challenge` (a defined-end programme: "7-day reset");
  - `event` (Eid, National Day, F1, school holidays: real dates);
  - `family`, `couples` (the call-out);
  - `value` (a **specific number**);
  - `proof`;
  - `local` (residents, a landmark).
  - Plus `brand` (hotel name or "official site") and `cta`.
- **Copy types:** short or long; new; reason-why ("because…"); benefits; status; "so that you can…"; describe, don't tell; real scarcity; real urgency; implied authority.
- **Image bank:** the scene at its best hour; contrast (inside the cool lobby vs the 45° street); people over rooms (Ogilvy); a real guest moment (with permission); the landmark; the plate the chef is known for; staff faces; a meme or "doodad" only for brands that can carry it.
- **Video bank:** phone-shot vs pro; subtitles always; a room or pool walk-through; a staff member talking to camera; guest UGC; a "what's included" flat lay; a countdown series for an event.

### What the 24 Oct 2026 backfill showed (Google, clean campaigns only; directional)
- **A specific number beats a vague promise.** "Save 50%… book direct" and "10% Off Direct Booking" won. "Best Rates Guaranteed" and "Exclusive Online Rates" got 0 conversions on 1,000+ clicks.
- **Generic CTA headlines lose** ("Book Your Stay": 3,377 clicks, 1 conversion).
- **Perks work as support, not as the lead.**
- Read the swipe file before choosing angles. Details: `6_Campaigns/ads_history/Learnings_24Oct2026.md`.

### Hormozi's 12 copy commandments (Gym Launch ch 3), the quick pass on every ad
1. Headline first; curiosity is king; never run without one.
2. Say what only you can say. The headline fails if any hotel could run it unchanged.
3. Call out who it's for, **and who it isn't** ("Not for the party crowd").
4. Give a reason why ("because…").
5. A **damaging admission** where true, ideally light ("We're 10 minutes from the beach, so the pool is never crowded"). It makes the rest believable.
6. Show, don't tell.
7. Tie benefits to status (how they'll be seen).
8. Urgency and scarcity only when legitimate.
9. Implied authority (a dated rating, a named award, the chef's background).
10. P.S. = a power sentence (emails and long ads).
11. Stupid-simple next steps (1, 2, 3).
12. Low reading level (FK, `scripts/fk_grade.py`).

## Email sequences
- **Soap-opera sequence** (Agora): episodic emails with open loops ("tomorrow I'll tell you…"). Good for a welcome series to new subscribers or members.
- **Pre-arrival bounceback [MH]:** within 24 hours of booking, send the upgrade, transfer, dining or spa offer. A second note 7 days before arrival covers practical information plus one more offer.
- **Abandoned booking gauntlet [MH]:**
  1. availability or urgency (real);
  2. reasons to choose and reviews;
  3. an alternative room or date at a similar price.
  - Use a discount only as a last resort. It cheapens the product (Agora).
  - Engine support is unverified.
- **Reactivation [MH]:** past guests at 11–12 months get an anniversary-of-stay note, then a "you goofed" style last chance (Agora). Expired loyalty members get re-invited.
- **Welcome / soap-opera for member sign-ups:** start with value, then the offer.
- **Every email gets:**
  - one idea;
  - one CTA;
  - a P.S.;
  - a subject line scored on the 4 U's;
  - an FK check;
  - **no dead ends**: a link to the next step.
- **Frequency:** Agora and the Obama 2012 campaign found more emails often raise revenue ("annoying emails work"). Test frequency against unsubscribes and complaints; don't assume.

## WhatsApp campaigns (client hotels) [MH]
Use opt-in lists only. Platform policy and local regulation apply (category: platform-policy/regulatory). Keep messages short, with one offer, one action and a real deadline, and identify the sender in the first line.

## Social content (Hormozi Core Four, "post free content")
- **Content unit = Hook → Retain → Reward.** Retain with lists, steps and stories. Reward means delivering what the hook promised; aim for value per second.
- **Give:ask** is about 4:1. Give in public, ask in private.
- **"How I", not "How to"** (experience is bulletproof): **[MH]** "How our chef sources…" rather than "How to choose a restaurant".
- Headline components that make news (Hormozi): recency, relevance, celebrity, proximity, conflict, unusual, ongoing. Use at least two.
- **Engagement devices (IKEA effect, Agora):** polls ("Which suite should we redesign?"), quizzes, a "pick your upgrade" choice.

## Lead magnets
- A **complete solution to a narrow problem that reveals the next problem**, which the core offer solves (Hormozi).
- Types: reveal a problem (an audit or diagnosis); a sample or trial; one step of a multi-step process.
- **Name tests matter most** (headline > image > subheadline). "How to…" names often win.
- **[MH] Hotel:** "The Dubai Family Weekend Planner" (email capture) → the family package.
- **[MH] B2B:** the Leak Audit; a "Direct-booking health check" of 3 questions (IKEA effect plus qualification).
- **Give away the secrets, sell the implementation.** Make it genuinely good, because it's the first impression (Bonner, "Special reports: forgoing the thud factor").

## Landing pages
- The page matches the ad's promise and look ("click to close" continuity).
- Ask for the minimum fields: every extra field cuts conversion (Bly).
- One CTA above the fold.
- Proof near the CTA.
- Photos with captions; captions are mini-ads (Ogilvy).
- Show price and inclusions clearly, and the total before payment.

## Customer-generated proof (Hormozi Marketing Machine)
Lifecycle asks for reviews and UGC right after a great moment (check-out, a compliment to staff). Award or feature the best guest stories (with permission). Run 1–2 events a year: a guest-appreciation evening generates content and testimonials.

## Newsjacking (Bonner, Agora)
Tie the offer to real news the same day (events, school holidays, a new metro line), and add a benefit or opinion. The facts must be verified on the day.

## SEO content (Agora; route lists and audits to `hotel-seo`)
- Answer real guest questions in a Q&A hub (Agora's Q&A Club model).
- Use trending topics daily (a morning research pass).
- Write in the searcher's words.
