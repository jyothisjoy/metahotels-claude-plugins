# Body copy, proof and close

Sources: Agora (notes 14, 16, 17, 18: MMF, Bencivenga, Halbert, Makepeace, Ogilvy, Hopkins); Hormozi's $100M Offers and Leads.

## Architecture of persuasion (MMF)
- **Lead** excites the heart.
- **Body** proves every claim to satisfy the doubting mind.
- **Close** shows the offer is great value and makes buying easy.

"The rational mind listens to reason only after the emotional mind has said okay."

## Reason-why (Bencivenga): every piece answers three questions
**Why you? Why true? Why now?** "50% off because of our fire" beats "50% off". Audit any weak piece against all three, including "better than doing nothing".

## Proof
- **Three kinds:** factual (data, research), anecdotal (a story), social (testimonials, endorsements).
- **Power of Three (MMF):** back each major claim with three proofs. Put the strongest first and longest, the next half as long, the third shorter. Vary the type.
- **Hotel proof hierarchy:** verbatim guest reviews with source and date → rating with source and date → awards (named, dated) → specific facts (minutes to the beach, sq m of the room, the dish the chef is known for) → generic claims (cut these).
- **What makes proof land** (Hormozi workshop, note 27 §3): raw beats polished (the phone video often beats the produced one); show beats tell; a guest who **looks like the reader and had their problem** (a family with toddlers, a business traveller on a late check-in); specific beats generic; recent; a **range** of results (someone wanting a quiet weekend doesn't believe only the honeymoon story); lots of it.
- **Weight the copy by why they book:** count how often each motivator appears in the last 50 reviews (data brief source 8) and give each its share of the page or video. Pain they want to escape usually beats the promise: Hormozi doubled bookings by switching from upside to pain.
- **Gates for collecting proof:** never offer anything for a *review* on Google, TripAdvisor or the OTAs (platform-policy, reputational). Incentivised UGC is disclosed (regulatory). Written consent for any guest's face or words. Prize competitions in the UAE may need a permit (regulatory).
- **Halbert's belief details:** a street address, not a PO box; a named contact; exact hours; "across from…" directions; references. Skepticism is the #3 reason people don't buy, after "don't want it" and "want the money more".

## Specificity = believability (Mike Ward, Makepeace, Ogilvy)
"Vague = unbelievable" (CUB). Swap atmosphere for figures and concrete scenes.
- "Luxurious rooms" → "48 sq m, floor-to-ceiling glass, and the Palm out of the bathtub."
- "Great location" → "7 minutes' walk to the Dubai Mall fountain."

## Chain of logic (Makepeace)
Start from a proposition the reader already believes. Add one link at a time, with proof at each step, so they keep nodding. Never let them feel lost. A muddled vision (promising one thing, delivering another) reads as deception.

## Long copy vs short (Bencivenga, Ogilvy, Bly)
Write for the motivated 5%, who want every detail before an important purchase. "Copy can never be too long, only too boring." Readership falls up to about 50 words, then barely drops to 500 (Ogilvy's data).

**Bly's Copy Length Grid:** long copy suits high-involvement, high-price, unknown or complex offers. Short copy suits low-involvement, impulse, well-known or simple ones. A weekend staycation landing page is medium. A wedding or MICE page, or a B2B case study, is long. A retargeting ad is short.

## Write it clean (Ogilvy, Zinsser, Strunk & White)
- Write the way you talk: short words, short sentences, short paragraphs. No jargon.
- **Zinsser's bracket test:** bracket every word not doing work. First drafts can usually be cut by 50%.
- Cut "due to the fact that" (because), "facilitate" (ease), "implement" (do), "it is interesting to note" (delete).
- **Don't be wishy-washy:** give a clear call.
- Nouns and verbs over adjectives. Don't overstate.
- **FK under 7.5** (MMF). Check with `scripts/fk_grade.py`.
- Never send on the day you write; read it aloud the next morning (Ogilvy). For urgent pieces, at least read it aloud once.

## Captions and visuals (Ogilvy)
Captions are read 2× more than body copy, so each is a mini-ad with the brand and the promise. Photos beat drawings. Photos with story appeal ("What's going on here?") beat posed ones. People beat products. Editorial layouts beat ad layouts.

## The close
- **Restate the offer so it gains allure**, not just repeating it (MMF's 12 questions).
- **A false close followed by a "liquidating" benefit:** "And one more thing…". Only use a real extra.
- **Risk reversal:** only what's real (free cancellation until X; best-rate guarantee if the hotel actually offers one).
- **One simple action**, spelled out: "Tap Book Direct, choose your dates, done in 60 seconds" (only if that's true of the engine).
- **P.S.** is the second-most-read line in an email. Put the strongest proof, the deadline, or the takeaway there.
- **Takeaway / disqualification** (Agora recruiting ads, MetaNex): "Not for you if you want the party pool." It works because it's honest. It can't be a false exclusivity.

## Hopkins and the scientific-advertising mindset (Agora Book Three)
- Key everything, so you know which piece did what. Unkeyed ads mean money spent blind.
- Hopkins's psychology:
  - price can signal quality;
  - personalisation sells;
  - offers framed as exclusive to a group pull;
  - invite comparison when you'd win it;
  - a bought sample is valued more than a free one.
- **The IKEA effect:** engagement devices (polls, quizzes, "pick your upgrade") make people value you more.
