# Big Idea, awareness and the copy platform

Sources: Agora Books One and Two (notes 14–17); Schwartz via Agora and Hormozi's GOATed Ads (note 06); Heath brothers via Agora.

## The Big Idea (MMF, from Ogilvy and Bonner)
- Ogilvy: "Unless your advertising contains a big idea, it will pass like a ship in the night."
- **Ogilvy's 5 tests:**
  1. Did it make me gasp?
  2. Do I wish I'd thought of it?
  3. Is it unique?
  4. Does it fit the strategy?
  5. Could it run for years?
- **Definition:** instantly understood as **important, exciting and beneficial**, leading to an **inevitable conclusion** that makes the product easy to sell. "The sale is half-made in the headline."
- **A big concept is not a big idea.** "Tourism is recovering" is a concept. "The guests who book you direct this season are guests you own forever" is an idea, because it carries the benefit and the conclusion.
- **Find the idea behind your idea.** Turn an assertion into something a reader would repeat at dinner.
- Examples that worked:
  - "How the French live longer… even though they eat like kings and smoke like chimneys" (a riddle plus an implicit promise);
  - *International Living*'s paradise scene with the twist "costs half as much as staying home" (the control for 20 years).
- **MMF's review rule:** every draft carries a one-sentence statement of its main idea at the top. If the sentence doesn't impress, the piece goes back unread.

## Rule of One (MMF)
One idea, one emotion, one story, one desired action. A list of so-so tips is a red flag: "if any one of the ideas were any good, it would deserve its own essay."

## Awareness levels → approach (Schwartz; Hormozi's hook mapping; Agora's lead types)
| Level | Reader state | Lead types | Hook style | [MH] hotel-guest example | [MH] B2B example (GM) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Most aware | Knows you, wants a deal | Offer | Offer-driven | "Past guests: your rate, plus breakfast, until 30 Nov" | "Your onboarding slot is ready" |
| Product aware | Knows you, unsure | Promise, proof | Proof-driven | "Why 1,000+ guests rated us 4.6 for families" (dated source) | Nearest register reference |
| Solution aware | Knows the kind of fix | Promise, problem-solution | Promise-driven | "Your JBR beach weekend, sorted: room, breakfast, late checkout" | "We run the demand side, not just host the rate" |
| Problem aware | Feels the pain | Problem-solution, secret | Pain-driven | "Summer in Dubai with kids? Here's an indoor weekend they'll love" | "Paying commission to re-acquire your own guests?" |
| Unaware | Not thinking about it | Story, proclamation, secret | Curiosity | A story or scene; a surprising local fact (sourced) | What Saif saw at the property; a sourced proclamation |

- Google Search ads are mostly *most aware* or *solution aware* (the searcher has intent). Meta and YouTube can go colder.
- An offer-led hook to a cold audience works only for known brands.
- If 90% of the hooks sit in "most aware", widen the spread.

## Market sophistication (Schwartz, via Agora and Hormozi)
When readers have seen every claim ("best rate guaranteed", "luxury redefined"), a bigger claim won't work. Move up a stage:
1. **A new mechanism:** *how* it works ("book direct and the minibar's on us").
2. **A new identity:** who it's *for* ("for families who hate the pool-chair race").
3. **A new experience:** sell the feeling or the scene, not the room.

## Removing objections by redefinition (Schwartz, Breakthrough Advertising)
- **Too complicated → simplify** into a few named, countable pieces.
- **Not important enough → escalate**: show that something big depends on it.
- **Costs too much → compare upward**: to a pricier standard ("less than one night at [comparable]", but only with a real comparison).
- **Concept judo:** flip a liability into an asset. Lifebuoy's medicinal smell became "the soap you can smell working". **[MH]** A "far from the Marina crowds" location becomes "the quiet side of JBR".

## Sticky ideas: SUCCESs (Heath)
Simple (a proverb, not a sound bite) · Unexpected (a curiosity gap) · Concrete (sensory) · Credible (let them test it themselves) · Emotional (one person, not a statistic) · Stories.

**Curse of Knowledge:** hotel people say "RevPAR" and "ADR", and guests say "a nice weekend". Write in the reader's concrete words.

## USP (MMF, Reeves, Ries & Trout)
Three characteristics:
1. the *appearance* of uniqueness;
2. usefulness;
3. conceptual simplicity.

Find the real, useful feature competitors don't mention, and own it. Never claim "only" without evidence.

## The copy platform (MMF): written before any copy
| Element | Write |
|---|---|
| Big Idea | One sentence |
| Big Promise | Benefit → deeper benefit → how they'll feel or be seen |
| Claims | 3–7 specific claims |
| Proof | For each claim: factual, anecdotal or social, with source and date |
| Credibility | Who stands behind it (the hotel's awards and reviews, the GM, MetaHotels' scale, register references) |

**Four-Legged Stool (MMF):** idea, promise, proof, credibility. Agora's credibility copy lifted response by 50%. Proof backs the *claims*; credibility backs the *people*.

## The Hierarchy of Value and Customer as Hero (Bonner)
Sell meaning, status and experience above function. The guest (or the GM) is the hero, and the hotel or MetaHotels is the guide.
