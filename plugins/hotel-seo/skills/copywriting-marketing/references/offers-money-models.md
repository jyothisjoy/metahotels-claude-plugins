# Offers and money models: for client hotels and for MetaHotels

Sources: Hormozi's $100M Offers, Money Models, Pricing, Lost Chapters, Fast Cash (notes 02, 04, 05, 10, 13); Agora Book Three, offers chapter (note 18: Bly, Sharpe, MMF, Vega).

**Gate:** every hotel offer must match the live rate, the hotel's cancellation policy, and its rate-parity and OTA contracts (contractual). Hotel management approves the offer. MetaHotels doesn't set a client hotel's prices.

## Value equation (Hormozi)
**Value = (Dream outcome × Perceived likelihood) ÷ (Time delay × Effort & sacrifice)**

The best offers shrink the bottom of the equation.

**[MH] For a hotel stay:**
- **Dream:** the experience and how they'll be seen (the anniversary photo, the kids' faces).
- **Likelihood:** reviews, photos of the actual room, a clear inclusions list.
- **Time:** instant confirmation; an easy transfer.
- **Effort:** everything included, nothing to arrange.

## Grand Slam steps (Hormozi)
1. Dream outcome.
2. List every problem in the order the guest meets them (planning, booking, arriving, staying, leaving). For each, consider the four doubts.
3. Turn each into a solution.
4. Delivery vehicles.
5. Trim and stack, then name each component.

Example. **[MH] Family staycation problems:** "Will the kids be bored?" → kids' club hours named. "Will we fight over pool loungers?" → reserved loungers. "Is breakfast a scrum?" → a family breakfast time slot. "Late checkout?" → included.

## M-A-G-I-C offer naming (Hormozi)
**M**agnetic reason why (Eid, Summer, National Day, a Grand Re-opening) · **A**nnounce the avatar ("Dubai Residents'") · **G**ive a goal ("Long-Weekend Reset") · **I**ndicate a time interval ("3 nights") · **C**ontainer word (Escape, Club, Package, Pass).

Example: "Dubai Residents' 3-Night Eid Escape". Rotate the wrappers each season, because local offers fatigue fastest.

When an offer fatigues, change things in this order: creative → body copy → headline/wrapper → duration → enhancer → monetisation structure (last).

## Enhancers (Hormozi), each with a hotel version and its honesty condition
| Enhancer | Hotel version | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Scarcity | Limited suites or rooms in the package | Real inventory allocation |
| Urgency | Book by [date] / stay by [date] | A real end date, which is then honoured |
| Bonuses | Breakfast, late checkout, spa credit, transfer, F&B credit | Real, deliverable, with a stated value only if it's the published price |
| Guarantee | Free cancellation until X; best-rate-direct promise | Must match the actual policy and engine behaviour |
| Name | M-A-G-I-C | — |

**Never discount the core; add a bonus instead.** It protects ADR and parity. "Only big discounts (≥50%) move people; 5–25% just cuts margin" (Hormozi, Lost Chapters). For hotels this is usually a reason to prefer added value over small discounts.

## Value propositions vs gimmicks (MMF)
**Durable value propositions:** BOGO, two-for-one, "stay 3, pay 2", "second room half price", free shipping on the best deal (hotels: free transfer on the best package), and genuine bonuses on better and best tiers.

Gimmicks work until they're copied. Spend 80% of creative energy on value propositions.

## Sharpe's 10 tests (run on every offer)
Specific · exclusive · valuable · unique · useful · relevant · plausible · easy to take up · urgent (real) · guaranteed (real).

## Bly's 6 lead-generating traits (for free offers and lead magnets)
Different · desirable · high perceived value vs cost · dramatises the USP · easy to take up (fewest form fields; every extra field costs conversion) · no risk or obligation.

## Pricing presentation
- **Rule of 100 (Berger, via Agora):** below about USD 100 (about AED 370), a % discount feels bigger; above it, an AED amount does ("AED 600 off your 3 nights"). This is inference for AED; test it.
- **Anchor high first** (Hormozi): show the suite or premium package first, then the core.
- **Smallest unit for display** ("per night", "per person"), **only if the total is shown before payment** (platform and consumer-protection category).
- **Good, better, best** with a two-step upgrade *before* checkout (MMF: people buy more when offered an upgrade by two degrees): room → room + breakfast → suite package.
- **Bly's sticker-shock moves (B2C-safe):** state the higher reference first (the real rack rate); split the value into components; add an element they can't easily price (a private experience).

## Money model for a hotel's direct channel (Hormozi stages → [MH])
| Stage | Hotel version |
|---|---|
| Attraction | Member or direct-only rate; a seasonal M-A-G-I-C package; a lead magnet (e.g. an "Eid in Dubai family guide" for email capture) |
| Upsell (moment of greatest need) | **Right after booking:** a pre-arrival offer of an upgrade, transfer, spa or dining. MMF's "buying frenzy" is strongest right after a purchase, so send it within 24 hours, not just before arrival. |
| Downsell | A non-refundable rate, a smaller room, or off-peak dates instead of discounting the same thing |
| Continuity | Loyalty tiers, a residents' membership or annual pass, prepaid staycation bundles (Recurring Max Cash) |

## Fast Cash Play for hotels (Hormozi; integrity flags apply)
A **quarterly**, limited, high-touch, high-ticket offer to the **warmest list** (past guests in the last 12–24 months). It costs no ad spend.
- Examples: a 10-suite NYE package with butler and transfer; a "Founders' Table" chef's dinner plus stay; a prepaid annual residents' pass.
- **Cap the number at what will definitely sell out.** Bonuses are released across the campaign.
- **Cut from the template:** "someone backed out", invented "X spots left", fabricated statistics. Use only real counts.
- Afterwards, collect the 4 Rs: Reviews, Results, Referrals, Resells.

## Bouncebacks, gauntlets and recapture (MMF and Vega, Agora Book Three)
- **Bounceback structures** at or just after purchase:
  - accessories (dining, spa);
  - an upgrade (room → suite);
  - a super-upgrade (an annual pass).
  - It lifts the average order about 25% (Agora's figure).
- **Abandoned booking recapture as a 3-email gauntlet**, not a discount:
  1. urgency (real availability);
  2. reasons and reviews;
  3. an alternative room or date at a similar price.
  - **Needs booking-engine support. Mirai's capability is unverified**, so check it first.
- **"You goofed" reactivation:** past guests from about 12 months ago get an anniversary-of-stay note with a reason to return.
- **Autorenew** (Agora): auto-bill memberships doubled profit. Disclose clearly; consumer-protection category.

## The hotel's direct channel as a wedding cake (Hormozi, Gym Launch ch 22–25) [MH]
- **Icing = one-off cash:** high-value packages and events (an NYE suite package, a wedding, a "summer family takeover").
- **Cake = recurring:** repeat-guest and member programmes, a residents' pass, prepaid staycation bundles. You need both: the cake holds when the icing melts (an ad account paused, a quiet season).
- **Sprinkles = internal offers** to in-house and past guests (spa, dining, upgrades, extensions). See `owned-demand.md`.
- **Tiers by buyer type, not by service.** Two tiers often beat three. At most three options; avoid the muddled middle ("If 20% pay 3×, you add 60% revenue while serving one-fifth").
- **Defined-end front-end package:** "long enough to believe a result, short enough to finish". Examples: a 3-night experience, a 6-week residents' wellness programme. Give it real dates and real capacity, with a continuity step after it (member rate, return-stay rate).
- **Splinter the inclusions** into a named list (what's already included, listed out). Only real inclusions, at published value.
- **Pre-arrival prescriptive upsell = the "second meeting":** after the booking, not inside the booking flow. Offer the piece that completes their stated trip purpose (spa for a couples' stay, transfers for a family). Customers buy far more readily than prospects.
- **Make each guest worth more to the hotel than to an OTA.** That's the case for paying more to acquire them directly.
- **Premium tier only once the core is near full.** "Exclusive" means nothing in a half-empty hotel.
- **Never:** inflated "was" prices or a reference price that never existed (regulatory: consumer protection); "only today" when it isn't.
